Primary external sources

FCC DA 26-786, 28 July 2026

Pages 1–4 and Appendix C, printed pages 11–15. Defines covered advanced robotic devices and foreign production. The latter references 48 CFR 25.101(a).

FCC DA 26-996, 18 September 2026

ANSCER AR250, AR650 and AR1250 received conditional approvals subject to the onshoring plan and updated product vetting. This approval does not extend to FlatHill.

FAR 25.101

U.S. manufacture and the general component-cost test. Cost must exceed 65% for deliveries in 2024–2028 and 75% starting in 2029. Review applicable exceptions, including COTS and iron/steel treatment.

FAR 25.003

Component and component-cost definitions, including treatment of unknown origin. Component cost excludes manufacturing the end product. Purchased and internally manufactured components have different costing rules.

Meta Muse Gadget SDK

Official Meta repository describing ESP32 and Linux support. Evidence of an engineering ecosystem, not a regulatory safe harbor.

Tesla 2025 Annual Report

Describes a global parts supply base. Does not establish a current percentage of China-origin parts in U.S.-built vehicles.

Tesla Q2 2019 Form 10-Q

Historical disclosure of U.S. vehicle production cost exposure to duties on components from China. Dated historical example only.

FTC Made in USA Rule

Unqualified Made in USA claims require all or virtually all U.S. content. A component-cost planning percentage does not itself support the marketing claim.

USPTO: Managing a patent

Patent rights exclude others. Registration does not grant a general right to make, use or sell a product and does not establish freedom to operate.

BOM assumptions and calculation

Every product’s assembly-group allocation totals 100%. Every illustrative domestic contribution totals 80 percentage points. These are proposed design budgets, not engineering estimates backed by quantities and quotes. Each underlying component must receive a supported origin classification. A group may contain multiple components, which explains partial contributions. Do not infer origin from the vendor’s address, ownership, chip brand or where an invoice was issued.

The model assumes an ordinary manufactured product cost test, no COTS waiver and no predominantly iron/steel classification. Confirm these assumptions for each SKU. Maintain a consistent direct-component boundary, avoid counting a parent assembly and its children twice, and exclude final end-product manufacturing costs from the component denominator. All manufacturing and test requirements remain independent release conditions.
