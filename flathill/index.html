Cost control starts before the factory.

A COMMON COST MODEL FOR EVERY RELEASED SKU
Landed parts + Build & test + Yield loss + Field support

Measure cost per good unit. Keep this operating-cost model separate from the origin-content calculation.

01

Fewer unique parts

Reuse actuator families, connectors and fixtures across products. Reduce tooling, inventory and redesign work.

02

Design for the process

Match tolerances to function. Standardize assembly sequence and end-of-line tests before scaling orders.

03

Measure the delivered cost

Track purchase cost, duties, yield loss, rework and warranty cost for each released configuration.

The economic goal: lower total delivered cost with repeatable quality.

Compliance is a product release discipline.

80%

Illustrative domestic component-cost target.
Supported by actual U.S. manufacture and an evidence file for each SKU.

01

Classify the finished product

The current FCC determination targets defined mobile robotic devices. Fixed industrial robots are excluded from that definition.

02

Validate manufacture and component cost

The referenced FAR test generally requires U.S. manufacture and domestic component cost above 65% through 2028, then above 75% from 2029. Exceptions require review.

03

Complete the authorization path

Covered List treatment and normal equipment authorization remain separate release checks. Conditional approval is a product-specific alternative where available.

PHASE 3 / LONG-TERM VISION
RSI

Recursive improvement of robot hardware and its manufacturing process

ROSE uses design, test and production outcomes to propose the next controlled revision.

  1. ObserveYield, torque, thermal behavior, failures and field returns
  2. ProposeDesign alternatives, process settings and approved part substitutions
  3. ValidateSimulation, bench testing and controlled pilot lots
  4. ReleaseEngineer approval, versioning, traceability and rollback
Success means lower cost per good unit and faster validated changes, with safety and quality held constant.

The 80% model has an explicit dependency.

QUADRUPED DESIGN SCENARIO
80 / 100 = 80%

Sum the cost of qualifying domestic direct components. Divide by the cost of all direct components.

Use one component boundary. Avoid counting both an assembly and its internal parts. Group totals in this deck are presentation rollups.

80%5 percentage points above the 75% comparison line.
01

Same denominator

Exclude final robot assembly cost, sales margin and corporate R&D from the component-cost denominator.

02

Evidence per component

Record manufacturer, manufacturing location, cost basis and origin support. Treat unknown origin as foreign.

03

A supplier address is insufficient

The 42-point joint contribution is conditional. If it fails, the illustrated domestic share falls to 38%.

FAR 25.003 and 25.101. Illustrative non-iron/steel-dominant, non-exempt cost scenario. No COTS waiver assumed.
